**Card 1** · _Reuters, US roadmap targets fusion deployment by mid 2030s_

**Q:** Distinguish fusion from fission across fuel type, reaction direction, and facility maturity.

**A:** Fission splits heavy atoms like uranium, while fusion combines light atoms together — the same reaction that powers the sun [S2]. Existing nuclear facilities rely on fission, but fusion devices are first-of-a-kind and the industry is still experimental [S2].
• Fuel: light atoms (fusion) vs heavy atoms like uranium (fission) [S2]

**Card 11** · _Reuters, US roadmap targets fusion deployment by mid 2030s_

**Q:** Why are neutron-resistant structural materials a central unsolved fusion challenge?

**A:** Fusion components must withstand intense neutron bombardment and extreme heat, and neutron-resistant structural materials remain an agreed, unsolved engineering barrier between experimental physics and commercial net energy gain [S2].

**Card 12** · _Reuters / Wikipedia, Fusion power_

**Q:** Why do future fusion reactors need breeding blankets?

**A:** Breeding blankets secure a steady supply of tritium, the nuclear fuel; the sources agree that future reactors are expected to rely on lithium blankets to breed their own tritium because tritium is scarce and decays [S2][S6].

**Card 14** · _Wikipedia, Fusion power_

**Q:** Distinguish gain factor above one, engineering breakeven, and economic breakeven across what each requires.

**A:** As of 2025 the National Ignition Facility is the only laboratory to have demonstrated a fusion energy gain factor above one [S6]. Efficiencies orders of magnitude higher are required for engineering breakeven (a net electricity-producing plant) and economic breakeven (net electricity pays the plant's whole-life cost) [S6].
• >1 gain achieved once (NIF); both breakevens remain unmet [S6]

**Card 15** · _Reuters, US roadmap targets fusion deployment by mid 2030s_

**Q:** What does 'net energy gain' require, and why haven't private developers reached it?

**A:** Net energy gain is the point where a fusion reaction produces more energy than was used to start and sustain it [S2]. Private developers have not yet achieved it, and the industry remains in an experimental stage [S2].
