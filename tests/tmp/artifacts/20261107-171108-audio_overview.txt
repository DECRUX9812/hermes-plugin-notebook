# Fusion 2026: money moved, physics didn't

_Audio overview of “Commercial Fusion Energy in 2026” — 23 segments, voices: en-US-AndrewNeural / en-US-EmmaNeural_

**Host A:** Okay so I spent the week inside six documents about commercial fusion and I came out with one sentence: the money changed hands, and the physics did not notice.

**Host B:** That's a good hook. Break it down, because 'money changed hands' sounds like a press release.

**Host A:** Fair. The Congressional Research Service says the US fusion energy sciences budget was seven hundred ninety million dollars in fiscal 2025. Congress then appropriated about eight hundred six million for the next year.

**Host B:** So roughly flat, with a rounding error of optimism.

**Host A:** Exactly. And against that, the Fusion Industry Association counted four point four eight billion dollars of private investment in the twelve months to July 2026. Fourteen point two four billion cumulative since 2021.

**Host B:** So the private sector is now outspending the public programme by a factor of five in a single year. That is the actual news.

**Host A:** There is a catch though. The CRS runs its own survey and gets nearly nine billion cumulative for 2021 to 2025. Same direction, half the total. They are counting different things.

**Host B:** Which is the sort of detail that decides whether a number is a fact or a slide.

**Host A:** Now the machine. ITER lowered its sixth sector module into the pit on the twenty-eighth of July 2026. Roughly eleven hundred tonnes, a thirty hour lift, and two thirds of the torus is now in place.

**Host B:** And that is ahead of schedule, right? I remember ITER being a synonym for delay.

**Host A:** About six months ahead on this campaign. The ninth module is planned for 2027. What improved is the assembly process, not the plasma physics.

**Host B:** So the part you can schedule got better. What about the part you can't?

**Host A:** Three things keep showing up. Structural materials that survive fourteen mega electron volt neutrons. A breeding blanket that makes its own tritium. And a supply chain that can qualify either one at scale.

**Host B:** And tritium is the quiet killer, isn't it? Civil inventory is measured in kilograms.

**Host A:** Right. Deuterium is seawater. Tritium you have to breed, in the blanket you have not built yet.

**Host B:** So when the roadmap says mid twenty thirties, what is it actually promising?

**Host A:** Pilot plants. And industry's own number for what that costs is more than a billion dollars a year for a decade. Congress appropriated seven hundred ninety million.

**Host B:** There's the gap, in two numbers.

**Host A:** And the cleanest finding in the whole set: the CRS says no defensible levelised cost figure exists yet. So anyone quoting you a price per megawatt hour is quoting a projection.

**Host B:** Three takeaways then?

**Host A:** One: private capital now sets the pace. Two: ITER's assembly is genuinely ahead, and that is an engineering win, not a commercial one. Three: materials, tritium and supply chain are unsolved and they are not on anyone's schedule.

**Host B:** And the thing to watch next is the 2027 appropriation, because the authorisation ceiling is a billion one and the cheque keeps coming in smaller.

**Host A:** That's the episode. Sources are in the notebook, citations on every claim.
