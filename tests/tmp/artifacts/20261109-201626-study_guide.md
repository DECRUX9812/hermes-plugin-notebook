## Mental model

Fusion energy is a three-stage conversion system — a fuel-and-machine input stage (tritium and light nuclei as fuel, a confinement device, neutron-resistant materials, and public plus private capital) feeds a transformation stage (a confined plasma burn releasing neutrons and heat, plus a lithium blanket that breeds its own tritium), which produces an output stage (heat intended for electricity, a self-renewing fuel cycle, and the test data and supply chain an industry needs) [S1][S2][S6].

- **Inputs — consumes/produces:** consumes tritium, described as a nuclear fuel [S2], light atomic nuclei that combine and release energy [S6], a machine assembled from sector modules [S3][S4], and money and policy direction from DOE and Congress [S1][S2]; produces a confinement device and a milestone-bearing program, e.g. DOE's roadmap with short, medium and long-term milestones [S2].
- **Transformations — consumes/produces:** consumes the plasma state, which requires high temperature, pressure and sufficient confinement time [S6], and the neutron flux that structural materials and plasma-facing components must survive [S2]; produces a burn at or near the ignition point of burning plasma [S1] and, in the blanket, a bred tritium supply in place of a purchased one [S2].
- **Outputs — consumes/produces:** consumes heat and, ultimately, the whole-life economics of a plant [S6]; produces electricity only at the far end of a ladder — scientific gain factor above one, then engineering breakeven (a net electricity-producing plant), then economic breakeven [S6] — while ITER itself is not designed or intended to produce electricity at all [S1].

**Stable ground (stated once):** the sources agree that ITER had installed six of nine tokamak sector modules by late July 2026, putting about two-thirds of the core in place [S3][S4]; that tritium is scarce and decays, so future reactors are expected to rely on lithium breeding blankets to breed their own [S2]; and that neutron-resistant structural materials are a central unsolved engineering challenge [S2]. On the ITER assembly numbers, S3 is coverage of an ITER Organization update, so S3 and S4 trace to one organization rather than corroborating each other [S3][S4].

## Concept cards

### Magnetic confinement

**Definition:** One of the technology approaches the CRS briefing uses to organize fusion R&D, alongside inertial confinement; ITER is the tokamak built under it, intended to prove the feasibility of fusion as a large-scale and carbon-free source of energy [S1].
**Mechanism:** Fusion fuel must be held as a plasma under high temperature, pressure and sufficient confinement time [S6]; in the tokamak, the core is assembled from nine sector modules, each combining a vacuum vessel sector, two toroidal field coils, thermal shields and auxiliary components [S3][S4].
**Failure mode:** Confinement is three coupled requirements rather than one, and the plasma edge remains the hard part — disruptive phenomena at the plasma edge are still an active research subject [S3][S6] — while no private developer has yet reached net energy gain [S2].
**Source:** [S1][S3][S4][S6]

### Burning plasma

**Definition:** The CRS briefing describes ITER's key goal as operating at or near the ignition point of burning plasma, called a critical step toward self-sustaining nuclear fusion [S1].
**Mechanism:** The supplied excerpt does not spell out the self-heating physics; what it does specify is that ITER pairs burning-plasma operation with extensive instrumentation for advancing understanding of plasmas and with opportunities for testing specialized materials [S1].
**Failure mode:** Success at burning plasma is a physics result, not a power result — ITER is primarily an exploratory science initiative and is not designed or intended to produce electricity [S1], so nothing in the electricity chain is demonstrated by it.
**Source:** [S1]

### Tritium breeding blanket

**Definition:** A blanket that breeds the plant's own tritium, the nuclear fuel, expected to be how future reactors secure a steady supply because tritium is scarce and decays [S2].
**Mechanism:** The sources state the requirement as an engineering deliverable rather than describe the reaction: DOE's roadmap sets milestones requiring companies to develop fuel processing technology alongside structural materials and plasma-facing components [S2]; the breeding reaction and any breeding-ratio arithmetic are not given in the excerpts.
**Failure mode:** A plant without breeding depends on an external supply of a fuel the sources describe as scarce and decaying [S2]; because no breeding ratio, inventory or doubling time appears in these sources, no quantitative self-sufficiency test can be constructed from them.
**Source:** [S2]

### Neutron-resistant structural materials

**Definition:** Materials and components — including plasma-facing components — that must withstand intense neutron bombardment and extreme heat inside a fusion device [S2].
**Mechanism:** The roadmap approach is to build infrastructure that closes the materials and technology gaps and to set milestones that force development of structural materials, plasma-facing components and fuel processing technology [S2]; ITER separately offers opportunities for testing specialized materials [S1].
**Failure mode:** The sources name this as one of the steep engineering challenges in moving from experimental physics to commercial net energy gain [S2], yet name no qualified material, no irradiation dose limit and no qualification schedule; ITER's material-testing role is described as an opportunity, not a qualification pathway [S1].
**Source:** [S2][S1]

### Net energy gain

**Definition:** The point where a fusion reaction produces more energy than was used to start and sustain it [S2].
**Mechanism:** S6 separates three rungs that are routinely collapsed into one word — a gain factor above one, engineering breakeven (a net electricity-producing plant), and economic breakeven (where net electricity pays the plant's whole-life cost) — and states that efficiencies orders of magnitude higher are required to move from the first to the others [S6].
**Failure mode:** Using "gain" as a single verdict. NIF is the only laboratory to have demonstrated a gain factor above one as of 2025 [S6], while private developers have not achieved net energy gain [S2]; a scientific-gain headline therefore says nothing about a power plant.
**Source:** [S2][S6]

### Tokamak sector module

**Definition:** The assembly unit of the tokamak core, composed of a vacuum vessel sector, two toroidal field coils, thermal shields and auxiliary components, nine of which make up the machine [S3][S4].
**Mechanism:** Modules are transferred from the Assembly Hall and lowered into the pit; the sixth module, approximately 1,100 tonnes and nearly 1,400 tonnes with its lifting rig, completed a 30-hour lift on 28 July 2026, and each successive module has gone faster as lessons carried into the next operation [S4].
**Failure mode:** The critical path migrates rather than disappears — vacuum vessel welding preparations began only once three sectors were confirmed stable on their gravity supports, and bevel repairs preceded work on sector 3 [S3], with the final module targeted for 2027 [S4]; module count is an assembly metric, not an operating date.
**Source:** [S3][S4]

## Trap alerts

**Novice assumption:** ITER is the first fusion power plant, so the assembly milestone means fusion electricity is next. / **What the sources show:** CRS states plainly that ITER is "primarily an exploratory science initiative and is not designed or intended to produce electricity" [S1]; its stated job is burning plasma at or near ignition and developing technology for a future demonstration plant [S1]. The six-of-nine, two-thirds-of-core figure is a construction metric on a machine that never had electricity generation in scope [S1][S4].

**Novice assumption:** Nobody has ever produced more fusion energy than they put in. / **What the sources show:** NIF is the only laboratory to have demonstrated a fusion energy gain factor above one as of 2025 [S6] — but that sits on the first rung only, since engineering breakeven (a net electricity-producing plant) and economic breakeven (net electricity paying whole-life cost) need efficiencies orders of magnitude higher [S6]. Two ledgers must be kept separately: scientific gain is demonstrated, private-developer net energy gain is not [S2][S6].

**Novice assumption:** The US fusion company count is settled at roughly 28–30, so it can be quoted either way. / **What the sources show:** S2 gives 28 US fusion companies, and the audit ledger records that figure as tied to a named Fusion Industry Association 2026 dataset; S3 says "around 30" [S2][S3]. The likely reading is one tally rounded at different moments rather than growth, but the sources do not settle the gap — quote 28 as the exact count and ~30 as an acknowledged rounding [S2][S3].

**Novice assumption:** Because DOE published a roadmap targeting deployment by the mid-2030s, the mid-2030s is a schedule. / **What the sources show:** The plan is a set of three goals — infrastructure to close materials and technology gaps, access to AI and advanced computing, and public-private partnerships for supply chain and workforce — plus milestones [S2]; but the same reporting says success "hinges on more federal funding, industry leaders say" [S2], and the named blockers are unsolved engineering (neutron-resistant materials, breeding blankets), not calendar items [S2]. The roadmap released in June and the Office of Fusion created in November 2025 coordinate a strategy whose funding is contingent [S2].

**Novice assumption:** Tritium is a fuel you buy, like uranium, and the blanket is an efficiency refinement. / **What the sources show:** Tritium is scarce and decays, so future reactors are expected to rely on lithium breeding blankets to breed their own supply [S2], and fuel processing technology is carried on DOE's milestone list beside structural materials and plasma-facing components [S2]. Fuel supply is therefore an in-plant subsystem that must be engineered and licensed, not a procurement line.
