## What the sources establish

**US roadmap targets mid-2030s** The DOE Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap, released in June, is the central plank of a federal strategy to deploy early-stage fusion power plants by the mid-2030s, coordinated by the Office of Fusion created in November 2025 [S2]. It sets concrete short-, medium- and long-term milestones for structural materials, plasma-facing components and fuel processing, while CRS records a parallel domestic track—PPPL's NSTX-U at 94% complete, first plasma anticipated in summer 2026 and pilot-plant operation targeted for 2035 [S2][S1].

**ITER assembly runs ahead** ITER installed its sixth of nine tokamak sector modules on 28 July 2026—almost six months ahead of schedule—placing two-thirds of the torus-shaped core in the pit [S4]. The 1,100-tonne module was lowered in a 30-hour lift, and the final module is now targeted for mid-2027 rather than December 2027 [S4].

**Private capital hits record, no gain** Fusion companies raised a record $4.48B worldwide in the 12 months to July 2026, lifting cumulative reported funding since 2021 to $14.24B, mostly from private investors [S2]. The IAEA's 2025 outlook still describes dozens of plant concepts at various stages of development worldwide and devotes its special focus topic to high-temperature superconductors and their potential role in commercialization [S5], while private developers have not achieved net energy gain [S2].

**Tritium and neutron damage bind** Tritium is scarce on Earth and decays with a half-life of about 12.3 years, so future reactors plan lithium breeding blankets that generate tritium under neutron radiation [S6]. Neutron bombardment also damages reactor inner walls, making neutron-resistant structural materials and breeding blankets the steep engineering challenges named by developers [S2][S6].

**Appropriation falls short of authorization** Legislation approved in 2022 authorized up to $1.1B for the DOE Fusion Energy Sciences program in FY2027, but Congress appropriated $790M—below the $805.7M enacted [S2]. A Realta Fusion executive told Reuters that “the plan isn't the problem” and that underfunding would let the commercialization timeline slip [S2].
