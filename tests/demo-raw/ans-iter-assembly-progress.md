# [ITER reports “substantial progress” in assembly and installation work](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-09/article-8375/iter-reports-substantial-progress-in-assembly-and-installation-work/)

Wed, Sep 9, 2026, 11:45 am[Nuclear News](https://www.ans.org/news/source-nuclearnews/)

![](https://www.ans.org/file/25269/coils-for-sm9-narrow.jpg)

Two toroidal field coils are transferred into the sector subassembly tool to join the vacuum vessel sector and its thermal shield for sector module 9 of the ITER project. (Photo: ITER)

In an update earlier this week, the ITER Organization [described](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/summer-substantial-progress) a “summer of substantial progress” as its teams work to advance multiple major assembly and installation activities for the lengthy and complex international fusion project.

In [May](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/soft-landing-giant-component), vacuum vessel sector 6 was transferred to its gravity support in the tokamak pit in a first-of-its-kind operation that ITER described as “an essential benchmark in vacuum vessel assembly.” Two additional vacuum vessel sectors were transferred during the summer, including sector 5 in August. ITER said that with “three sectors now resting on their gravity supports, teams have confirmed the configuration is sufficiently stable to begin preparations for vacuum vessel welding.”

Following completion of bevel repairs and other preparatory activities, assembly teams have begun working on vacuum vessel sector 3. Technicians installed instrumentation on the outer shell and welded bosses on the inner shell of this sector, which, according to ITER, is “one of the most heavily instrumented vacuum vessel sectors, carrying 120 sensors that will be used to measure magnetic field during machine operation.”

In July, sector module 1 of the tokamak was lifted and positioned safely. It was the sixth of nine sector modules—each composed of a vacuum vessel sector, two toroidal field coils, thermal shields, and auxiliary components—to enter the tokamak pit. With this operation, two-thirds of the plasma chamber are now in place, and that the last of the Korean-manufactured vacuum vessel sectors is installed.

In August, work advanced with sector module 9, the vacuum vessel component of which was positioned into the sector subassembly tool in June. Thermal shields had also been put into the subassembly tool. With the addition of the two toroidal field coils in August, the subassembly of sector module 9 can now be completed, which is expected to happen by the end of November. Sector module 9 is scheduled for transfer to the tokamak pit in early December.

ITER machine assembly program manager Jens Reich said that the “work on sector module 9 is particularly important because it is on the critical path. We need to move the completed module to the tokamak pit so the next and last sector can take its place in the sector subassembly tool. We’re dealing with tight sequences but at this point we are happy with the progress.”

Additional progress this summer included repair work on the vacuum vessel thermal shield, in which microscopic stress-corrosion cracks had been discovered in 2021. This July saw the completion of a carefully planned three-year repair project, in which approximately 11 kilometers of cooling pipework was removed and 1 metric ton of silver coating was ground away to eliminate risks from chlorine residues. New piping was then welded to the thermal shield panels, and the panel surfaces were polished to restore their ability to reflect thermal radiation.

Installation work for the fusion project’s system of 93 bellows has also begun. The bellows system will connect the vacuum vessel to the cryostat and the cryostat to the seven-story tokamak building. The initial installation is focusing on the rectangular port cell bellows, five sets of which are now in place. The welding project that includes the bellows is scheduled to last for five years.

On September 7, ITER [reported](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/ramping-testing) that it took “another step towards full-current trials of select superconducting magnets” by beginning preparations “to test a 330-\[metric ton\] toroidal field coil at 34 kA, more than three times the current achieved during the launch of operations at ITER’s magnet cold test facility earlier this year.”

According to ITER, “further upgrades to the ITER magnet cold test facility, including additional structural reinforcement, are planned before the start of 68 kA testing, which will begin with toroidal field coil #19.”

**ITER timeline:** The progress achieved this summer is a boost to the ITER Organization as it works toward its [current goals](https://www.iter.org/few-lines) of beginning research operation in 2034, achieving full magnetic energy in 2036, and starting deuterium-tritium operation in 2039.
